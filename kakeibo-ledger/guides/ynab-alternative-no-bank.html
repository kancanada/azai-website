Kakeibo vs YNAB: a YNAB alternative without bank linking

Updated 15 November 2026 · 6 min read
Short answerYNAB doesn't actually require bank linking: you can use it with unlinked accounts and enter transactions yourself. What it does require is a subscription (US$14.99 a month or US$109 a year on 15 November 2026, after a 34-day free trial) and an account, with your data in YNAB's cloud. Kakeibo is a lighter alternative: a monthly plan, four spending categories and a month-end reflection, done on paper or in an app. Pick YNAB if you manage several accounts, debts or a shared household budget; pick kakeibo if you mainly want to see and change where your spending goes.

We make Kakeibo Ledger, a kakeibo app for Android that we mention near the end. We haven't used YNAB for this article; everything about YNAB and Goodbudget comes from their own published pages, linked below.

People usually search for a YNAB alternative for one of three reasons: the price, the bank connection, or the feeling that YNAB is more system than they need. Kakeibo addresses all three, but it isn't a like-for-like replacement. This guide explains what each approach does, what it costs, and when YNAB is still the better choice.

First, a myth: YNAB doesn't require bank linking

YNAB's own help pages describe two ways to get transactions in. You can link an account for Direct Import, which YNAB says works through the data providers MX and Plaid, or you can leave an account unlinked and enter transactions by hand, which YNAB calls the tried-and-true method. You can also download a file from your bank and import it.

So if bank linking is your only objection, you can keep YNAB and simply not link. What you can't avoid is the subscription, the YNAB account, and your budget living on YNAB's servers so it can sync between your phone and the web.

If you do link, YNAB says that banks supporting OAuth let you sign in directly with the bank, so you don't give your banking password to the data provider; with other banks you may need to enter your login credentials. YNAB says it stores transaction details (date, payee, amount) and some account details such as name and balance.

What YNAB is

YNAB (You Need A Budget) is zero-based budgeting software. You assign every dollar you currently have to a category before you spend it, and when you overspend in one category you move money from another. It tracks account balances, credit cards and loans, handles irregular “true expenses” by setting money aside for them every month, and syncs between iOS, Android and the web so a household can share one budget.

It's a thorough system, and many people credit it with getting them out of debt. It also asks for a lot of attention: categories to fund, accounts to reconcile, and a learning curve that YNAB supports with its own guides.

What kakeibo is

Kakeibo is a Japanese household ledger method dating from 1904. You plan the month (income, fixed costs, savings goal), write every expense in one of four categories (needs, wants, culture, unexpected), and answer four reflection questions at month end. There are no account balances to reconcile and no money to move between categories. It's a spending journal with a plan at the start and a review at the end. What YNAB costs over time

At US$109 a year, three years of YNAB comes to US$327, or about US$540 if you pay monthly. If YNAB is what keeps you out of credit card debt, that's good value. If you mainly wanted to see where your money goes, it's a lot to pay for a spending diary.

Kakeibo on paper or in a spreadsheet costs nothing. Kakeibo apps range from free with ads to one-time purchases to subscriptions; we compare them in the best kakeibo apps for Android.

When YNAB is the better choice

  • You're paying down several debts or credit cards and need to see balances and plans for each.
  • You share a budget with a partner and want both of you to see the same numbers live.
  • You have big irregular expenses (annual insurance, car registration, holidays) and want to fund them a little each month in their own categories.
  • You like detail and want to budget on a computer as well as a phone.
  • You're happy for your budget to live in a company's cloud in exchange for sync.

When kakeibo is the better choice

  • You earn enough but don't know where it goes, and you want to notice spending rather than manage accounts.
  • You don't want a subscription for budgeting.
  • You don't want an account, or your financial data on anyone's server.
  • You've tried YNAB and found the system more work than the problem.
  • You want a monthly ritual of reflection, not just a running total.

Other YNAB alternatives that don't need bank linking

Goodbudget

Goodbudget is a digital version of the envelope system. Its free plan, which it says is free forever, includes 10 regular and 10 “more” envelopes, one account, two devices and a year of history, with manual entry. Premium is US$10 a month or US$80 a year and adds unlimited envelopes and accounts, five devices, seven years of history and automatic bank sync, which Goodbudget lists for US banks only. Your budget syncs through Goodbudget's servers, so it needs an account. It's a good middle ground if you want envelopes and partner sharing without YNAB's price.

Cashew

Cashew is a free, open-source expense and budget tracker for Android, iOS and the web. Its Google Play listing says your data stays on your device unless you turn on optional Google Drive backups, and its Data safety label says no data is collected or shared. It has flexible budgets, custom categories, multiple currencies and recurring bills. It's a great choice if you want a general-purpose tracker; it doesn't follow the kakeibo method.

Questions

Can you use YNAB without linking your bank?

Yes. YNAB lets you keep accounts unlinked and enter transactions by hand, or import a file downloaded from your bank. You still need a YNAB subscription and account.

How much does YNAB cost?

On 15 November 2026, YNAB's pricing page listed US$14.99 a month or US$109 a year, with a 34-day free trial and a free year for eligible college students.

Is kakeibo a good alternative to YNAB?

For people who mainly want to understand and change their spending, yes: it is simpler and can be free. For managing several accounts, debts or a shared household budget, YNAB does more.

What is the cheapest YNAB alternative?

A kakeibo notebook or spreadsheet costs nothing. Among apps, Cashew is free, Goodbudget has a free plan with 20 envelopes, and some kakeibo apps are free with a one-time upgrade.

Which bank data providers does YNAB use?

YNAB's help pages name MX and Plaid as the providers for linked accounts. With banks that support OAuth you sign in directly with your bank; with others you may need to enter your login credentials.
