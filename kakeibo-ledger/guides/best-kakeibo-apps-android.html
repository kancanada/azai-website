The best kakeibo apps for Android in 2026

Updated 15 November 2026 · 6 min read
Short answerIf you want the full kakeibo method on Android, four apps stand out. Kakeibo Ledger (ours) is free with a one-time US$7.99 Pro and has no internet permission. Kakeibo: Budget Planner by Halil Apps has the most free features, including widgets, CSV export and a no-spend calendar, with an optional Pro subscription. Kakeibo: Budget & Expense Log is a one-off US$2.99 purchase with stats and a payday start. Kakebo by RellowTeam is the most downloaded but is a general expense tracker with ads rather than the kakeibo method.

We make Kakeibo Ledger, one of the apps in this comparison, so weigh our view accordingly. We haven't done hands-on testing of the other apps; their details come from their Google Play listings on 15 November 2026, linked below. Data safety labels are written by each developer.

Search Google Play for “kakeibo” and you'll find a dozen apps with the name. Some follow the method closely: a monthly plan, four categories and a month-end reflection. Others borrow the word for a general expense tracker. Neither is wrong, but if you came for kakeibo, it helps to know which is which.

Here's how we compared them, what each one offers, and which we'd suggest for different people, including when ours isn't the best choice.

What makes a good kakeibo app

  1. It follows the method. A monthly plan with savings set first; the four categories (needs, wants, culture, unexpected); and a month-end reflection with the four questions.
  2. Entry is fast. Manual entry is the point of kakeibo, so it has to take seconds, not a form.
  3. It respects your data. No bank login required, clear Data safety label, ideally no account.
  4. Fair pricing. The core method shouldn't sit behind a paywall, and you should know what you're paying for.
  5. You can get your data out. Backup and restore at minimum, export ideally.

Kakeibo Ledger

Best for: people who want the method as it's taught, with the strongest privacy guarantee and no subscription.

Our app. You set up the month with income, fixed costs and a savings goal, and it works out what's left to spend and suggests a split across the four boxes (50/25/10/15, which you can change). Entries take two taps after the amount, with Today and Yesterday shortcuts and an optional note. At month end it fills in the first three questions and asks you for the fourth; a streak counts reflected months. There's an optional Sunday check-in.

Privacy is the main difference. The app doesn't request Android's internet permission, so it can't go online at all; you can check that in its permission details on Google Play. No account, no ads, no analytics. Backups are free and encrypted with a passphrase you choose.

Free covers the whole method with the last three months of history. Pro is US$7.99 once and adds unlimited history, a year in review, app lock, CSV export and two extra themes.

Limits: it's brand new, so there are no reviews yet. The four categories are fixed. One currency, no widgets, no sync, and months follow the calendar rather than your payday. CSV export is a Pro feature.

Kakeibo: Budget Planner (Halil Apps)

Best for: people who want the most features free, including widgets.

A well-designed kakeibo app with the method at its centre: you set savings first, split the rest across four pillars, log expenses and reflect monthly with the four classic questions plus a journal. The free tier is generous. Its listing includes home-screen widgets for remaining budget and quick add, a spending calendar with no-spend days and streaks, CSV export, dark mode, every currency and a daily reminder. A newer feature can prompt you to categorize Google Wallet payments, using notification access you choose to turn on.

Pro is an optional subscription with a free trial for eligible users, and adds a monthly coaching report, month-over-month trends, a recurring-expense finder and CSV import. The listing says financial records stay on your device, with no bank linking or cloud sync; its Data safety label declares no data shared but says the app may collect app activity, app performance and device IDs, and mentions that a payment service processes an anonymous customer ID for subscriptions.

Choose it over ours if you want widgets, free CSV export, a no-spend calendar or a wider choice of currencies, and you don't mind a subscription for the extras.

Kakeibo: Budget & Expense Log (Pablo Muñoz García)

Best for: people who'd rather pay a small amount once and get everything.

The closest app to ours in philosophy. It follows the plan, track, reflect cycle with the four classic categories, a monthly budget wizard, a copy-last-month option and a guided five-step reflection. It adds features we don't have: weekly, monthly and yearly statistics, emoji tags across categories, search and filters, 27 currencies, CSV and full SQLite export, and plans that can start on your payday. Its listing says it's fully offline with no account, and its Data safety label says no data is collected or shared.

It costs US$2.99 up front, with no free version to try first, and it has a very small user base so far (50+ downloads).

Choose it over ours if you want stats, tags, payday-based months or several currencies, and you're happy to pay before trying.

Kakebo: Track Expense & Budget (RellowTeam)

Best for: people who want a familiar, flexible expense tracker and like the kakeibo name.

The most downloaded app for the keyword, with 100K+ downloads and a 4.5-star rating. It's a capable expense and budget tracker: income and expenses, accounts, categories, recurring transactions, savings goals, charts and offline use. Reviewers include a traditional kakeibo notebook user who liked its real-time balance and PIN lock.

It doesn't follow the method closely: there's no fixed four-category structure or month-end reflection in its listing, and some categories and transaction types require the premium upgrade, according to reviews. It shows ads, and its Data safety label says it may collect financial info.

Choose it if you want flexibility and a large, established app more than the kakeibo routine.

Not a kakeibo app, but worth knowing: Cashew

If what you really want is a private, free budget tracker, Cashew is hard to beat. It's open source, rated 4.8 stars from about 28,000 reviews, and its Data safety label declares no data collected or shared. It doesn't follow the kakeibo method, but you could set up four categories yourself. See budget apps that don't link to your bank for more options like it.

Which should you pick?

  • Strict method, maximum privacy, pay once: Kakeibo Ledger.
  • Most free features, widgets, no-spend tracking: Kakeibo: Budget Planner (Halil Apps).
  • Stats, tags, payday months, one small payment: Kakeibo: Budget & Expense Log.
  • A flexible general tracker with the kakeibo name: Kakebo (RellowTeam).
  • No app at all: a notebook and our free kakeibo template.

Whatever you choose, the app matters less than the routine: write things down the same day, look at the totals once a week, and do the reflection at month end.

Questions

What is the best kakeibo app for Android?

It depends on what you value. Kakeibo Ledger follows the method strictly and has no internet permission; Halil Apps' Kakeibo: Budget Planner has the most free features; Kakeibo: Budget & Expense Log is a one-time US$2.99 purchase with stats and payday months; RellowTeam's Kakebo is the most downloaded general tracker.

Is there a free kakeibo app?

Yes. Kakeibo Ledger and Halil Apps' Kakeibo: Budget Planner are both free to use, with optional paid upgrades. RellowTeam's Kakebo is free with ads and in-app purchases.

Is there a kakeibo app that works offline?

Several. Kakeibo Ledger has no internet permission at all. Kakeibo: Budget & Expense Log says it is fully offline, and Halil Apps and RellowTeam both say your expense data works offline or stays on your device.

Is there a kakeibo app for iPhone?

Kakeibo Ledger is Android only. There are kakeibo apps on the iPhone App Store, but we haven't compared them in this guide.

Do kakeibo apps link to your bank?

The kakeibo apps compared here don't link to banks; you enter expenses yourself, which is the point of the method. Halil Apps' app can optionally read Google Wallet payment notifications if you turn that on.
