How it works

Plan. Write. Reflect.

The three steps of kakeibo, and nothing else. Setup takes about a minute and there's nothing to link.

01

Plan the month

Enter your take-home pay, your fixed costs and what you want to save. The app works out what's left to spend and suggests a split across the four boxes. Next month starts from this one.

02

Write it down

Tap a box, type the amount, tap Save. Two taps for most expenses, with an optional note. Typing it yourself is the point: it's what makes you notice.

03

Reflect at month end

The four kakeibo questions, with the numbers already filled in. You write the one thing you'll change, and your streak of reflected months grows.

Four boxes

Every expense goes in one of four boxes.

No sub-categories to manage. Each box shows what you've spent against what you planned, so you can see at a glance which one is running hot.

必

Needs

Rent, groceries, bills, transport. What you'd pay for in any month.

欲

Wants

Eating out, shopping, fun. Nice to have, and where most months go over.

文

Culture

Books, courses, museums, hobbies. Spending that makes you more you.

臨

Unexpected

Repairs, medical, gifts. The things no plan sees coming.

Month-end reflection

Four questions. One honest look back.

  1. How much did you have?
  2. How much did you want to save?
  3. How much did you spend?
  4. How can you improve?

When a month ends, Kakeibo Ledger asks you to close it. It fills in the first three answers from your plan and your entries; the fourth is yours. Next month, your note is right there when you plan.

Privacy

No internet permission. Check for yourself.

Most budget apps want your bank login. Kakeibo Ledger doesn't even ask Android for permission to use the internet, so it can't send your numbers anywhere. You can see this in the app's permission details on Google Play.

There's no account, no analytics and no ads. Your ledger is stored in the app's private storage, kept out of Android's cloud backup, and deleted if you uninstall.

FAQ

Questions

What is kakeibo?

Kakeibo (家計簿, "household ledger") is a Japanese budgeting method first published by Hani Motoko in 1904. At the start of the month you note your income, fixed costs and savings goal. During the month you write down every expense in one of four categories: needs, wants, culture and unexpected. At the end of the month you answer four questions: how much you had, how much you wanted to save, how much you spent, and how you can improve.

Does Kakeibo Ledger connect to my bank?

No. There is no bank sync, receipt scanning or import. You type each expense yourself, which is the point of kakeibo: writing it down is what makes you notice where the money goes.

Can the app really not go online?

Yes. Kakeibo Ledger doesn't ask Android for the internet permission, so it can't open a network connection. You can check this in the app's permission details on Google Play. Purchases still work, because the app asks the Google Play app on your phone, and Google Play does the talking to Google.

How do I back up my ledger?

In Settings, choose Backup and pick a passphrase. The app encrypts your whole ledger with AES-256-GCM and opens Android's share sheet, so you can save the file to Google Drive, email it to yourself or keep it in Files. Restore reads the file back on any phone. Backups are free. Without the passphrase nobody can open the file, including us.

Is Kakeibo Ledger free?

The whole method is free, with the last 3 months of history and encrypted backups. Pro is a single one-time purchase of US$7.99, not a subscription. It adds unlimited history, a year in review, app lock, CSV export and two extra themes. There are no ads.

Which currencies does it support?

Twenty, including the US, Canadian, Australian and New Zealand dollars, the euro, the pound, the yen and the rupee. You pick one when you start and can change it in Settings.

Is there an iPhone version?

No. Kakeibo Ledger is available for Android 8.0 and later.
