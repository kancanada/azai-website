What Read AI does

Read AI describes itself as a meeting assistant that turns meetings into decisions and follow-ups. Its main features, as listed on its site:

  • Online meeting notes. Read joins Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams meetings and produces transcripts, summaries, action items and, on higher plans, video.
  • Meetings you skip. Read can summarize a meeting you didn't attend.
  • In-person meetings. The Read AI app for iPhone (iOS 17.5+) and Android (Android 12+) records face-to-face conversations with one tap, up to four hours each. You need a free Read account, and meetings sync to your other devices.
  • Beyond meetings. Read also summarizes email and messages, and its "Ask Read" and Search Copilot answer questions across your meetings, email and messages.
  • Meeting coaching. Even the free plan lists a personalized meeting coach and topic readouts.
  • Integrations. Paid plans connect to Notion, Salesforce, HubSpot, Jira, Confluence, Zapier and webhooks.

How Read AI handles privacy and consent

Read's privacy page makes several commitments:

  • Data is encrypted with AES-256 at rest and TLS 1.2+ in transit.
  • AI training is opt-in, and off by default.
  • Read doesn't sell user data to advertisers or other third parties.
  • Read announces itself at the start of every meeting and requires host approval before joining.
  • If any participant opts out, that meeting's data is immediately and permanently deleted.

That's a clear approach for online calls. For in-person recordings, there's no bot to announce itself, so telling people you're recording is up to you, and the law may require it. See our guides to US meeting recording consent laws and choosing a private AI note taker.

Like Fireflies and Otter, Read is a cloud service: your meetings are processed and stored by Read, and you need an account. For most meetings that's fine. For confidential ones, some people prefer a tool that keeps the audio on the device.

Who Read AI suits

Based on what Read publishes, it looks strongest for:

  • people whose work is split between online meetings, email and chat, and who want one assistant across all three;
  • teams on Zoom, Meet and Teams who want summaries pushed to Notion, Jira, Salesforce or HubSpot;
  • anyone who wants meeting coaching and metrics;
  • iPhone and Android users who want in-person and online notes in one account.

When an on-device app makes more sense

If most of your meetings are in a room, and some are confidential, the trade-off changes. VoiceVault records through your phone's microphone, then writes the transcript, names who said what, and produces a summary and a to-do list, all on the phone. There's no account and no VoiceVault server, so we never receive your recordings. It works in airplane mode, and there are no minute limits.

It does less than Read in other ways: it's Android 13+ only, it doesn't join Zoom or Teams calls or record phone calls, it doesn't summarize email, and speaker labels apply within one recording rather than across meetings.

Read AI review: the bottom line

Read AI covers a lot of ground: online meetings, in-person recording, email and messages, coaching and integrations, with a clear opt-out for participants. The free plan is enough to try it, but 5 transcripts a month and a 1-hour limit won't cover a busy week, and playback and compliance features sit on the higher tiers. If you want one cloud assistant across your work, it's worth a trial. If you want meetings written up on your own phone and kept there, look at an on-device app instead.
