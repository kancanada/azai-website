"Works offline" means different things in different apps. Some record offline but transcribe later on a server. Some transcribe offline but send your audio to the cloud for summaries. This guide sorts the Android options by what you actually want to do, and gives you a quick test to check any app yourself.

What makes speech-to-text work offline

Turning speech into text needs a speech-recognition model. Cloud apps keep that model on their servers and send your audio there. Offline apps download the model to your phone, usually once, over Wi-Fi, and run it on the phone's own chips. After that first download, they don't need a connection, and your audio doesn't leave the phone.

Dictation: Gboard

Google's keyboard can recognize speech on the phone once you've downloaded your language in its voice-typing settings (menu names vary by version). It's ideal for messages and short notes, not for recording a meeting.

Live captions: Live Transcribe

Live Transcribe shows what people are saying in real time, which is excellent for accessibility. According to Google, on Android 12 and newer you can download several languages for offline use; on Android 8 to 11, English. It's built for following a conversation as it happens rather than producing meeting notes.

Meeting recordings: built-in recorders

If you have a Pixel, Google Recorder transcribes on the phone and is free. On recent Samsung Galaxy phones, the Voice Recorder app can transcribe with Galaxy AI; check Samsung's settings if you want summaries processed on the phone as well.

Meeting notes on any Android phone: VoiceVault

VoiceVault downloads NVIDIA's Parakeet speech model (25 European languages) and an AI for summaries the first time you open it on Wi-Fi. After that, the transcript, the speaker labels, the summary and the to-dos are all made on the phone, and it works in airplane mode. There's no account and no VoiceVault server.

Test any app in two minutes

  1. Turn on airplane mode, and make sure Wi-Fi is off too.
  2. Open the app and record 30 seconds of normal speech.
  3. Stop. If the transcript appears now, transcription is on the phone.
  4. Ask for a summary, if the app makes one. If it fails or waits for a connection, summaries use a server.
  5. Turn airplane mode off and check whether anything uploads or syncs.

Do this test with the app you already use. It's the fastest way to know whether a "private" or "offline" claim holds up.

What offline costs you

  • A one-time download. Speech and summary models are large; set things up on Wi-Fi.
  • Phone power and time. Your phone does the work, so long meetings take longer to summarize on older phones.
  • Fewer cloud extras. No bots joining online meetings, and no shared team workspace.

In return you get transcripts anywhere, nothing on anyone else's server, and no per-minute pricing. For the cost side, see AI note takers without a subscription.
