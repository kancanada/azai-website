If you're reading Plaud Note reviews, you probably want to know three things: what it does, what it really costs once you add Plaud's AI plans, and whether you need it at all. This isn't a hands-on review; we haven't used the device. It's a plain summary of what Plaud itself publishes, with the alternatives side by side, so you can decide what to test.

What the Plaud Note is

The Plaud Note is a recorder about the size of a credit card: 3.37 by 2.13 inches, 0.12 inches thin, and 1.06 oz, according to Plaud. It has two modes:

  • In-person mode records the room with two microphones, with an effective range Plaud lists as 9.84 feet.
  • Phone call mode records calls. You attach the Note to the back of your phone with its magnetic case, and a vibration conduction sensor picks up the sound from the phone's speaker.

You press and hold to record and press briefly to highlight a moment. Plaud lists up to 30 hours of continuous recording, 60 days on standby and 64 GB of storage. Recordings go to the Plaud app over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi, where they're transcribed in 112 languages with speaker labels and summarized using one of more than 10,000 templates. You can add typed notes and photos for context, and ask questions across your recordings with Ask Plaud. Plaud also has a desktop app for recording online meetings with a Plaud device.

Where your recordings go

The Note records on the device, but the AI work happens in Plaud's cloud. Plaud's Trust Center says "all data is transferred to the cloud for processing". It also says:

  • audio and transcripts aren't stored in the cloud unless you turn on Cloud Sync; otherwise they're deleted from the cloud once they're back on your phone;
  • data is processed in Plaud's data centers in the US, Frankfurt, Singapore or Japan, and its AI providers are contractually bound not to keep or train on it;
  • Plaud doesn't train on your data unless you opt in;
  • data is encrypted in transit and at rest, and the device is tied to your Plaud account.

Plaud lists ISO 27701, GDPR, SOC 2 Type 2, HIPAA and EN 18031 compliance. That's a serious set of commitments. The practical point is simpler: to get a transcript, you need a connection, a Plaud account, and to be comfortable with the audio passing through a cloud service. If you'd rather it didn't, see AI notes for confidential meetings.

Who the Plaud Note suits

  • People who record a lot of phone calls. This is the Note's clearest advantage over phone-only apps, and over the wearable NotePin, which Plaud says doesn't record calls.
  • People who want one button. Plaud's point is that you don't have to unlock your phone and open an app.
  • Long days away from a charger. Thirty hours of recording is a lot.
  • Multilingual work, with 112 transcription languages listed.

It's less of a fit if your meetings are mostly online (a bot such as Otter's joins those for you), if you don't want another device to carry and charge, or if your audio shouldn't leave your phone.
