A voice recorder with transcription saves you the hour after the meeting. Instead of replaying audio to find what was agreed, you search the text, copy the decision and move on. On Android you have four serious options, and the right one depends mostly on which phone you own and where you're comfortable having your meetings processed.

The short list

  • Google Recorder: the default choice if you have a Pixel.
  • Samsung Voice Recorder with Galaxy AI: the built-in option on supported Galaxy phones.
  • Otter: a cloud service, best when your meetings are mostly on Zoom, Meet or Teams and your team shares notes.
  • VoiceVault (ours): in-room meetings on any Android 13+ phone, with the transcript, summary and to-dos made on the phone.

Each app in a paragraph

Google Recorder

If you carry a Pixel, try this first. Google has said from launch that Recorder transcribes on the device, so it works without a connection, and you can use it without a Google Account. Pixel 8 and later can summarize a transcript; Google's help page says summaries run in a secure cloud environment and work for recordings between 5 minutes and an hour. The catch: it's Pixel only, and speaker labels are US English only.

Samsung Voice Recorder with Galaxy AI

On a supported Galaxy, Transcript Assist turns recordings into text, translates the transcript and writes a summary, inside the Voice Recorder app you already have. Samsung's own notes say it requires a network connection and a Samsung account login, and that audio files must be under 3 hours. It's a sensible free starting point if you're already on a recent Galaxy and are comfortable with Samsung processing the audio.

Otter

Otter is the strongest choice when most of your meetings are online. It can join Zoom, Google Meet and Teams, and teams can share, highlight and comment on notes. Its Android listing says it needs an account and an internet connection to transcribe, and that your data backs up to Otter's cloud. The free plan suits occasional use; heavy users will hit the monthly minute caps. For the trade-offs in detail, see our guide to an Otter alternative that works without the cloud.

VoiceVault

VoiceVault is built for the meeting in the room: a one-on-one, a client visit, a board session. Tap Record and, when you stop, the phone writes the transcript with a name on each voice, a short summary and a to-do list with an owner on each item. There's no account and no VoiceVault server, so recordings never leave the phone unless you back them up to your own Google Drive. It's free for 30 days, then a one-time purchase.

Where it's not the right pick: it's Android only, it doesn't join Zoom or Teams, and it doesn't record phone calls. Speaker names apply within one recording, so you name voices again in the next meeting.

What about Fireflies and other meeting bots?

Tools such as Fireflies are built mainly to join online meetings from your calendar, as their Android listing describes. They're useful for video calls but are not really phone voice recorders, so we've left them out of the table.

How to choose

  1. Mostly online meetings? Use a cloud service with a meeting bot, such as Otter.
  2. Mostly in the room, and you have a Pixel or recent Galaxy? Try the built-in recorder first. It costs nothing extra.
  3. Confidential conversations (HR, legal, clients, board)? Prefer transcription on the phone with no account, so nothing sits on a vendor's servers. See how to choose a private AI note taker.
  4. Tired of monthly plans and minute caps? Compare the cost over a year in AI note takers without a subscription.
  5. Need to-dos, not just text? Check whether the app writes a summary and action items, or only a transcript.

Tips for a recording that transcribes well

  • Tell people you're recording. It's courteous and often required. See recording consent laws in the US.
  • Phone flat in the middle of the table, microphone end uncovered, every speaker within a few steps.
  • Keep it away from noise: laptop fans, projectors, air vents and coffee cups.
  • Turn on Do Not Disturb so notification sounds don't land in the recording.
  • Check names and numbers before you share a transcript or summary. Every app gets some wrong.
