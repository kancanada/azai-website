Minutes are the official memory of a meeting. Done well, they settle "what did we agree?" in ten seconds. Done badly, they're a wall of text nobody reads. The difference is mostly about what you leave out.

What minutes are for

Minutes exist so that people who were there agree on what happened, and people who weren't can catch up in a minute. That means they record outcomes: decisions, actions and owners. They are not a transcript. If someone needs the exact words, that's what a recording is for.

What to include

  • Meeting name, date, time and place (or call link).
  • Attendees, and apologies from people who couldn't come.
  • Each agenda item, with one to three lines on the discussion and, more importantly, the outcome.
  • Decisions, stated plainly: "We will launch on 18 November."
  • Action items, each with one owner and a due date. See how to write action items people actually do.
  • Next meeting, if there is one.

What to leave out

  • A blow-by-blow account of the discussion.
  • Who said what in passing, unless it's a formal decision or vote.
  • Jokes, side conversations and tangents.
  • Opinions that didn't lead to a decision.

Template

Copy this into your notes app or document and fill it in.

[Meeting name] Date: [day, date] · Time: [start–end] · Place: [room or link] Chair: [name] · Notes: [name] Present: [names] Apologies: [names] 1. [Agenda item] Discussion: [one to three lines] Decision: [what was agreed] 2. [Agenda item] Discussion: [one to three lines] Decision: [what was agreed] Action items - [Action] — Owner: [name] — Due: [date] - [Action] — Owner: [name] — Due: [date] Next meeting: [date, time]

Example

Q4 launch plan Wednesday 28 October · 9:30–10:00 · Room 4 Chair: Anna · Notes: Daniel Present: Anna, Priya, Daniel 1. Beta results Discussion: 42 testers; support tickets down by a third since the last build. Decision: Beta is complete. 2. Launch date Discussion: Design sign-off is the only open dependency. Decision: Launch moves up to 18 November, if store screenshots are signed off by Friday. Action items - Confirm the launch budget with finance — Owner: Anna — Due: today, 4 pm - Sign off on the store screenshots — Owner: Priya — Due: Friday - Send the press note draft to the team — Owner: Daniel — Due: Wednesday Next meeting: Wednesday 4 November, 9:30

Tips that make minutes useful

  1. Write decisions as sentences. "Launch on 18 November" beats "launch date discussed".
  2. One owner per action. "Marketing" or "the team" means nobody.
  3. Send within a day, and ask for corrections by a set time.
  4. Keep a recording if your organization allows it. When someone disputes the minutes, you can check what was actually said.
