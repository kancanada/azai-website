Fireflies is one of the most popular AI note takers for online meetings. People look for a Fireflies alternative for different reasons: a bot in every call, client discomfort, cloud storage, the cost per seat, or meetings that happen in a room rather than on Zoom. This guide explains how Fireflies works, then matches alternatives to each reason, including when Fireflies is still the best pick.

How Fireflies works

Fireflies' main tool is a notetaker bot. You invite it to meetings on your calendar, and it joins calls on Google Meet, Zoom, Teams, Webex and other platforms, then captures audio and video and writes a transcript and summary. Fireflies also has a Chrome extension that captures Google Meet without the bot, and a mobile app that records in-person conversations. Everything is processed and stored in Fireflies' cloud.

On its security page, Fireflies says it encrypts transcripts at rest (256-bit AES) and in transit, that it doesn't train AI on your data by default, and that it is certified for SOC 2 Type II, GDPR and HIPAA. HIPAA support and private storage at a location you choose are Enterprise features.

Why people look for a Fireflies alternative

  • A bot in every meeting. Some clients and colleagues don't like an extra participant recording the call, and some organizations block note-taking bots.
  • Consent. Recording laws differ by state and country. A bot is at least visible in the call, but you still need to know whose consent you need. See our guide to US meeting recording consent laws.
  • Cloud storage. For HR, legal, medical or board conversations, some people would rather the audio never reach a third party's servers.
  • Cost per seat. A five-person team on Business pays $95 a month billed yearly, or $145 billed monthly.
  • In-person meetings. Bots can't join a meeting room. Fireflies' mobile app can record one, but it still goes through the cloud.

Fireflies alternatives by need

You want another cloud note taker: Otter or Read AI

Otter is the closest like-for-like alternative: it joins online meetings and also records on the phone. On 3 November 2026 its free Basic plan included 300 minutes a month with a 30-minute limit per conversation; Pro was $16.99 a month or $8.33 a month billed yearly, with 1,200 minutes and a 90-minute limit. Read our Otter alternative guide for how it handles your audio.

Read AI joins Zoom, Meet and Teams, and also summarizes email and messages. Pro was $19.75 a month or $15 billed yearly. We cover it in Read AI: what it does, what it costs, and alternatives.

You want fewer bots: Fathom

Fathom's pricing page lists a free plan with unlimited recordings and transcriptions, and a choice of bot capture or bot-free capture (bot-free is in beta, on Mac). Premium was $20 a month or $16 billed yearly; Team plans start at $19 per user ($15 billed yearly) with a two-user minimum. Fathom's site mentions an iPhone app but no Android app.

You already pay for Google, Zoom or Microsoft: use the built-in notes

  • Google Meet "Take notes for me" writes notes into Google Docs and tells everyone in the meeting that notes are being taken. It needs an eligible Google Workspace edition or Google AI plan.
  • Zoom's AI assistant (now called ZoomMate on Zoom's site) includes meeting summaries for 3 hosted meetings a month on the free plan; the paid tier lists unlimited AI note-taking.
  • Microsoft Teams intelligent recap gives AI notes and suggested tasks after a meeting. It needs a Teams Premium or Microsoft Copilot license.

The advantage: no extra vendor or bot, and the data stays with a company you already trust with your meetings. The limit: each works only inside its own platform.

Your meetings happen in a room: an on-device phone app

For meetings around a table, a phone in the middle does the job without any bot. On Android, Google Recorder transcribes on the phone on Pixels. Samsung Voice Recorder with Galaxy AI transcribes on recent Galaxy phones, though Samsung says its Transcript Assist needs a network connection and a Samsung account. VoiceVault works on any phone with Android 13 or newer.

VoiceVault records through your phone's microphone, then writes the transcript, names who said what, and turns it into a summary and a to-do list, all on the phone. There's no account and no VoiceVault server, so we never receive your recordings, and it works in airplane mode. It's free for 7 days, then US$49.99 once, with no minute limits.

The trade-offs: it's Android only, it doesn't join Zoom or Teams calls, and it doesn't record phone calls. Speaker labels apply within one recording; it doesn't recognize the same voice across meetings. For more on keeping meetings off the cloud, read our guide to a private AI note taker.

When Fireflies is still the best choice

Fireflies does a lot that phone apps and built-in notes don't. Stay with it if:

  • most of your meetings are online, across several platforms, and you want one place for all of them;
  • you want notes pushed to Slack, Notion, Asana or your CRM;
  • you coach a sales or recruiting team with talk-time, sentiment and topic tracking;
  • your team needs shared channels, soundbites and search across everyone's meetings.

Many people end up with two tools: a cloud note taker for online calls and an on-device app for the meetings in the room. If that's you, see how to transcribe an in-person meeting on Android.
