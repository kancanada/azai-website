Otter is one of the best-known AI note takers, and for good reason: it's accurate, it joins online meetings for you, and teams can share and comment on notes. But it works in a particular way that doesn't suit every meeting. This guide explains what Otter does with your audio, when that's a problem, and which Android apps work differently.

How Otter handles your meetings

Otter's own Google Play listing spells it out. Live transcription works "when online", the requirements include an "internet connection for transcribing" and "account creation", and your data is automatically backed up to Otter's cloud. In practice that means:

  • You need a signal to get a transcript. You can record on a plane or in a basement meeting room, but the words appear only after the audio reaches Otter's servers.
  • Your meetings live on someone else's servers. Otter says it doesn't sell or share your data and that you can delete it. For many meetings that's fine. For performance reviews, HR matters, legal or medical conversations, some people and some employers would rather the audio never leave the phone.
  • You pay by the minute, in effect. On 31 October 2026, Otter's free Basic plan included 300 transcription minutes a month with a 30-minute limit per conversation. Pro listed 1,200 minutes a month and a 90-minute limit, at $16.99 a month or $8.33 a month billed yearly. Business removed the monthly minute cap for meetings, with a 4-hour limit per conversation.

When Otter is still the better choice

An on-device app isn't better at everything. Stay with Otter (or Fireflies) if:

  • most of your meetings are on Zoom, Google Meet or Teams and you want a bot to join and record them;
  • your team shares notes, comments and highlights in one workspace;
  • you use an iPhone, or need your notes on the web;
  • you want slides captured automatically from online meetings.

When an on-device app makes more sense

  • Confidential conversations. Board prep, HR, negotiations, a doctor's appointment. If the transcript is made on your phone, there's no copy on anyone's server.
  • Patchy or no internet. Client sites, flights, trains, older office buildings.
  • Long or frequent meetings. No minutes to ration and no per-conversation cap.
  • You'd rather not pay monthly. On-device apps don't run servers for every minute you record, so they can be sold once.

What VoiceVault does differently

VoiceVault downloads its own AI to your phone once, over Wi-Fi: NVIDIA's Parakeet model for transcripts, and Google's Gemma (or your phone's Gemini Nano) for summaries. After that, recording, transcripts, speaker labels, summaries and to-dos are all made on the phone, and work in airplane mode. There's no account and no VoiceVault server, so there's nothing for us to read, share or sell. Backups go to your own Google Drive, and only if you turn them on.

The trade-offs are real: it's Android only, it doesn't join online meetings, and the AI download is large, so set it up on Wi-Fi. Summaries of long meetings take longer on older phones.

How to switch from Otter

  1. Export anything you want to keep from Otter: transcripts as PDF, TXT or SRT, audio as MP3.
  2. Install VoiceVault from Google Play and open it on Wi-Fi so it can download its AI.
  3. At your next meeting, tap Record and put the phone in the middle of the table. The screen can be off.
  4. When you stop, the transcript, summary and to-dos appear on the phone. Tap a speaker label to put a name to each voice.
