A Forest app alternative for Android: five options compared

Updated 15 November 2026 · 6 min read
Short answerIf you like Forest's tree but not its ads, Flora is the closest match: same grow-a-tree idea, no ads according to its listing, and group focus with friends. If you want the free, built-in route, Android's Focus mode pauses distracting apps without installing anything. If you want study groups, try YPT. If you want a simple timer with no ads, no account and no data collected that only runs while your phone is face down, that's Flip to Focus, which we make. Forest is still the most polished option if you're happy with its model.

We make Flip to Focus, one of the alternatives below. Everything about Forest and the other apps comes from their own Google Play listings and websites, checked on 15 November 2026.

Forest has been one of the most popular focus apps since 2014, with more than 10 million downloads on Google Play and an Editors' Choice badge. It works for a lot of people. But it's also changed over the years, from a one-time Pro purchase to a Plus subscription, with ads in the free version, and people search for alternatives for different reasons. Here's what Forest offers today, the common reasons to switch, and the options that fit each one.

What Forest does well

Forest's idea is simple and effective: when you want to focus, you plant a seed. It grows into a tree while you stay off distracting apps, and withers if you give in. Over weeks, your sessions become a forest you can see. Its Google Play listing (checked 15 November 2026) also includes:

  • An allowlist so essential apps stay usable during a session
  • Time Guard, which blocks chosen apps on a schedule using Android's accessibility service
  • Plant Together rooms with friends, where everyone's tree withers if anyone uses their phone
  • Tags, a timeline, and weekly, monthly and yearly stats
  • Over a hundred tree species and soundtracks to unlock
  • Real trees: Forest partners with Trees for the Future, and says its users have planted over 2 million trees with in-app coins

It's also on iPhone and has a Chrome extension, which matters if you study across devices.

Why people look for a Forest alternative

Ads and upselling

The Android app is listed as "Contains ads". Forest's own site says ads appear occasionally in the free tier, never during an active focus session, and that Plus removes them. Some reviewers mention feeling pushed toward Plus.

The move from Pro to Plus

Forest used to sell a one-time Pro upgrade. Its site now says Pro is no longer available to new users (existing Pro users keep it), and the paid tier is Forest Plus, a subscription with three times the coin rewards, exclusive species and real-tree planting. In-app purchases on Google Play range from $0.99 to $59.99 per item. If you prefer to pay once, that's a reason to look elsewhere.

Data and permissions

Forest's Data safety label says it may share personal info with third parties and may collect location, personal info and five other data types. Time Guard needs accessibility access to see which app is open. That's normal for an app blocker, but some people would rather not grant it.

The game stops working

For some people the tree is motivating for a few weeks and then becomes background. If you find yourself killing trees without caring, a different mechanism may help more than a different tree.

1. Flora: the closest like-for-like

Flora by AppFinca uses the same core idea: plant a tree, and it dies if you leave the app. It adds a to-do list that turns tasks into a journey of plants to collect, a garden that grows with consistency, stats across days, weeks and months, and groups where friends focus together and compare time. Its Play listing says "No ads". Real-tree planting comes with a Flora Care subscription. On Android it's much smaller than Forest (100K+ downloads), with a 4.6★ rating. See our Flora alternative guide for its trade-offs.

2. Android's Focus mode: free and already installed

Open Settings, then Digital Wellbeing, and you'll find Focus mode on most Android phones. Choose the apps that distract you and pause them with one tap or on a schedule. There's no tree, no timer and no stats beyond Digital Wellbeing's screen-time and unlock counts, and it's easy to turn off. But it's free, has no ads, and covers the core of what Forest's blocking does.

3. YPT: if you want study partners

YPT is built around studying with other people: groups where you can see friends' status, real-time rankings in your exam category, subject-by-subject tracking, a planner and an app blocker. If Forest's Plant Together was the part you liked, YPT goes much further. It shows ads, and its Data safety label lists location and personal info among data that may be shared.

4. FLIP: flip timing with a community

FLIP by RinaSoft times study while your phone is face down, then adds focus levels, detailed stats, a D-day countdown, to-do lists, live "who's studying" counts and school rankings. Like YPT, it's ad-supported, and its Data safety label lists messages among collected data and says data isn't encrypted.

5. Flip to Focus: no ads, no account, pay once

We built Flip to Focus for people who want the focus without the game, the feed or the data collection. Instead of blocking apps, it makes the phone itself the commitment: the timer only runs while the phone is face down, and lifting it pauses the session. Do Not Disturb turns on automatically, the screen switches off, and each session gets a focus score out of 100 that drops when you pick the phone up. Optional Strict Mode ends the session if you hold the phone for more than 10 seconds.

It also has subjects with daily goals, an exam countdown, a 5, 10 or 15-minute break timer, weekly and monthly stats, streaks with a year-long heatmap, a home-screen streak widget, reminders and CSV export. There's no account and no server, and its Data safety label says no data collected or shared. It's free for 7 days, then a one-time US$9.99 (US$4.99 if you buy on day one).

What it doesn't have, compared with Forest: no trees or rewards to unlock, no real-tree planting, no app blocking or allowlist, no friends or rooms, no iPhone app and no browser extension.

When to stay with Forest

  • The tree genuinely motivates you, and you'd miss your forest.
  • You use an iPhone or Chrome as well as Android.
  • You need to block specific apps while keeping others available.
  • You study with friends in Plant Together rooms.
  • You value the real-tree planting.

If you're mainly bothered by notifications and the habit of picking up your phone, any of these tools can help, and a few habits help more than any app. See how to stop checking your phone while studying.

Questions

Is there a free alternative to Forest?

Yes. Android's Focus mode, in Settings under Digital Wellbeing, pauses chosen apps for free with no ads. Forest itself is also free to download, with ads and optional purchases.

Is there a Forest alternative without ads?

Flora's Google Play listing says it has no ads, and it uses the same grow-a-tree idea. Flip to Focus and Android's Focus mode also have no ads.

Can I still buy Forest Pro?

According to Forest's site, the one-time Pro purchase is no longer available to new users. Existing Pro users keep it, and the current paid tier is the Forest Plus subscription.

Does Forest plant real trees?

Yes. Forest partners with Trees for the Future, and you can spend in-app coins to plant real trees. Its listing says users have planted over 2 million.

Is Flip to Focus like Forest?

Both help you stay off your phone, in different ways. Forest blocks apps and grows a tree. Flip to Focus only times your session while the phone is face down and pauses when you pick it up. It has no trees, rewards or social features, but no ads, no account and a one-time price.
