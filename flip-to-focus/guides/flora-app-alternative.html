A Flora alternative for Android: what to use instead, and when to stay

Updated 15 November 2026 · 6 min read
Short answerFlora is a good app: ad-free according to its listing, with a grow-a-tree timer, a to-do list and groups for focusing with friends. Look elsewhere if you want something simpler, need to keep using your phone for study apps during a session, or want a timer that collects no data at all. Forest is the bigger, more feature-rich tree app (with ads). YPT is better for study groups. Android's Focus mode is free and built in. Flip to Focus, which we make, is a no-account timer that only runs while the phone is face down.

We make Flip to Focus, one of the alternatives below. Everything about Flora and the other apps comes from their own Google Play and App Store listings, checked on 15 November 2026.

Flora – Green Focus, by AppFinca, has been around since 2016 and calls itself the original multi-user tree-planting focus app. On iPhone it's very popular; on Android it's smaller, with 100K+ downloads and a 4.6★ rating on Google Play. If you're weighing it up or looking for something different, here's what Flora does, the common reasons to switch, and alternatives that suit each reason.

What Flora does

From its Google Play listing (checked 15 November 2026):

  • Tree-growing focus sessions. Plant a seed before you start; it grows while you stay focused. Leave to check social media or games and the seedling is killed.
  • To-dos as an adventure. Enter your tasks and Flora turns them into a journey of plant discovery around the world; finishing tasks unlocks plants for your garden.
  • A garden and stats. Your garden fills in as you build consistency, with stats for focus time, habits and trends across days, weeks and months.
  • Groups. Create a study group or team, focus together, share tasks, compare weekly or monthly time and share gardens.
  • Real trees. With a Flora Care subscription, focused hours fund real trees, and you can back a group so members without a subscription contribute too. Its App Store listing names partners in Africa and East Asia, including Trees.org and Eden Reforestation Projects.
  • No ads, according to its listing.

It's free to download, with in-app purchases from $0.99 to $134.99 per item on Google Play.

Why people look for a Flora alternative

You need your phone during study

Flora's tree dies if you leave the app. That's the point, but it's awkward if you study with a dictionary app, flashcards or lecture videos on the same phone. A tool that blocks only specific apps, or a timer that doesn't care which app is open, may suit you better.

You want less, not more

Flora mixes a focus timer, a to-do list, a habit garden and a social layer. If you already plan in a notebook or another app, you may only want the timer.

Data

Flora's Google Play Data safety label says it shares no data with third parties but may collect location, personal info and five other data types. Its App Store privacy label lists usage data as used to track you, and purchases, contact info, identifiers and usage data as linked to you. That's common for an app with accounts and social features, but some students prefer an app that collects nothing.

Cost of extras

Real-tree planting needs a subscription, and Flora's in-app purchases go up to $134.99 per item on Google Play. If you'd rather pay once, check what you actually need before buying.

Forest: the bigger tree app

If you like the tree but want more depth, Forest is the obvious move. It has an allowlist so study apps can stay open during a session, scheduled app blocks, over a hundred species to unlock, Plant Together rooms, detailed stats, an iPhone app and a Chrome extension. The trade-offs: the Android app shows ads in its free version, the paid tier is now a Plus subscription, and its Data safety label lists personal info as possibly shared with third parties. See our Forest alternative guide.

YPT: for serious study groups

Flora's groups are friendly and small-scale. YPT is built around groups: see friends' study status live, compare rankings in your exam category, track time per subject, and block apps during sessions. It suits long exam preparation with a group of classmates. It shows ads.

Android's Focus mode: the free minimum

If the tree has stopped doing anything for you and you just want certain apps off-limits, Digital Wellbeing's Focus mode does that for free on most Android phones, on demand or on a schedule. It has no timer and no motivation layer, so pair it with a simple timer.

Flip to Focus: a timer that collects nothing

Flip to Focus, which we make, takes a different approach. It doesn't block apps or grow trees. You flip the phone face down to start; Do Not Disturb turns on and the screen switches off; pick it up and the session pauses. Each session gets a focus score out of 100 that drops when you pick the phone up, and optional Strict Mode ends the session if you hold it for more than 10 seconds.

It has subjects with daily goals, an exam countdown, a 5, 10 or 15-minute break timer, weekly and monthly stats, streaks and a year-long heatmap, a streak widget, reminders, a shareable study-time image and CSV export. There's no account and no server: its Data safety label says no data collected or shared. It's free for 7 days, then a one-time US$9.99, or US$4.99 if you buy on your first day.

Compared with Flora, it has no garden or trees, no real-tree planting, no to-do list, no groups and no iPhone version. Because the phone has to be face down, it doesn't suit studying with apps on that same phone either.

When Flora is still the best choice

  • You want a tree app without ads.
  • Your tasks and your focus sessions live in one place, and the garden keeps you coming back.
  • You focus with friends or a small team and like comparing progress.
  • You want your focus to fund real tree planting.
  • You switch between iPhone and Android.

The app is only part of it. A clear task, a silenced phone and a planned break do most of the work; our guide to the Pomodoro Technique shows how to set that up.

Questions

Is Flora free?

Flora is free to download with no ads, according to its Google Play listing. It sells in-app purchases from $0.99 to $134.99 per item, and real-tree planting needs a Flora Care subscription.

Is Flora better than Forest?

They share the same idea. Flora has no ads according to its listing, and combines focus with to-dos and groups. Forest has more features, including an allowlist and a Chrome extension, but shows ads in its free version. Forest is much bigger on Android.

Is Flora available on Android?

Yes. Flora – Green Focus is on Google Play, with 100K+ downloads and a 4.6-star rating on 15 November 2026. It's much more widely used on iPhone.

Does Flora collect data?

Its Google Play Data safety label says no data is shared with third parties, but it may collect location, personal info and five other types of data. Its App Store label lists usage data as used for tracking.

What's a Flora alternative that doesn't need an account?

Flip to Focus has no account and collects no data, according to its Data safety label. Android's Focus mode also works without signing up for anything.
