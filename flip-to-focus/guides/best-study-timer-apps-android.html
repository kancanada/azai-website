The best study timer apps for Android, compared

Updated 15 November 2026 · 7 min read
Short answerIt depends on what pulls you away. If you study better with other people watching, YPT or FLIP have the strongest live study groups and rankings. If you need rewards and a game, Forest is the most polished. If you want something free and built in, Android's Focus mode pauses chosen apps. If you want a simple timer with no ads, no account and no data collected, that only counts while your phone is face down, that's what we built Flip to Focus for. The trade-off is that it has no social features.

We make Flip to Focus, one of the apps compared here, so read our verdict with that in mind. Everything about the other apps comes from their own Google Play listings and websites, checked on 15 November 2026; we haven't run hands-on tests of every feature.

Search Google Play for "study timer" and you'll get hundreds of results, most of them a countdown clock with ads. A few stand out because they actually change how you use your phone while you study, by blocking apps, growing a virtual tree, putting you in a room with other students, or only counting when the phone is face down. Here are the ones worth considering, what each does, what it costs and what it collects.

Forest: best for gamified focus

Forest is the best-known focus app. You plant a seed at the start of a session and it grows into a tree while you stay away from distracting apps; leave, and it withers. Over time you build a visual forest of your study history. It has an allowlist so essential apps stay usable, a "Time Guard" for scheduled blocks (it uses Android's accessibility service to see which app is open), custom tags, weekly to yearly stats, and "Plant Together" rooms where the whole group's tree withers if anyone uses their phone. Forest partners with Trees for the Future, and its site says users have planted over 2 million real trees by spending in-app coins.

Good for: people motivated by rewards and visuals, and groups of friends. Watch out for: ads in the free version, a paid Plus subscription for some features, and reviews from people who bought the older one-time Pro version and feel pushed toward Plus. More in our Forest alternative guide.

FLIP: best for social, flip-style studying

FLIP by RinaSoft is the app that popularised flip-to-study timing: turn the phone face down and it measures study time. Around that core it has a lot: a focus level based on breaks versus focus time, statistics by day, week and month, a timeline and automatic timetable, a daily report, a D-day exam countdown, a to-do list and study schedule, one-tap sharing, a "LIVE" count of people studying now, school rankings and a global study board. Its mini-window lets you keep timing while using a dictionary or watching a lecture.

Good for: students who like studying "alongside" others and comparing hours. Watch out for: ads, and a Data safety label that lists messages among collected data and says data isn't encrypted in transit.

YPT: best for study groups and subject tracking

YPT (Yeolpumta) by Pallo has more than 5 million downloads on Google Play. It's a stopwatch-style tracker by subject, with a Pomodoro mode, an app blocker during sessions, a daily planner and to-do list, study groups where you can see friends' status, real-time rankings in your exam category, colour-coded stats and a Wear OS companion. Its 4.7★ rating is the highest here.

Good for: long exam campaigns where you want to track hours per subject and feel part of a group. Watch out for: ads, and social features that share your study activity with others.

Flora: best ad-free tree app with friends

Flora by AppFinca is the other well-known tree-growing focus app; it launched on the App Store in 2016. Leave the app during a session and your tree dies. It has a to-do list that turns tasks into a "journey" of plants to collect, stats across days, weeks and months, and study groups. Its listing says it has no ads. Real-tree planting comes with a Flora Care subscription. More in our Flora alternative guide.

Good for: people who like Forest's idea but not its ads, and friends who study together. Watch out for: in-app purchases up to $134.99, and a much smaller Android following than on iPhone.

Android's Focus mode: best free option

Most Android phones already have Digital Wellbeing in Settings. Its Focus mode pauses the apps you choose with one tap, or on a schedule, and the dashboard shows how many times you unlocked your phone. It has no timer, stats or study features, and you can turn it off in a second, but it's free, ad-free and already there. Many students use it alongside a separate timer.

Flip to Focus: best for no ads, no account, no data

We built Flip to Focus because most popular study timers pay for themselves with ads, accounts or data. It does one thing: the timer only runs while your phone is face down, and picking it up pauses the session. Do Not Disturb turns on automatically, the screen switches off to save battery, and an optional Strict Mode ends the session if you hold the phone for more than 10 seconds.

Around that, it has subjects with daily goals, an exam countdown (D-12, D-11…), a focus score out of 100 per session, a 5, 10 or 15-minute break timer, weekly and monthly stats, streaks with a year-long heatmap, a home-screen streak widget, focus-schedule and streak reminders, a shareable study-time image and CSV export. There's no account and no server; Google Play's Data safety label says no data collected and none shared.

Good for: students who want a simple, private timer and a clear record of focused time. Watch out for: no study groups, rankings or live counts; no app blocker; no to-do list; Android only. It's free for 7 days, then a one-time US$9.99 for lifetime access (US$4.99 on your first day).

Questions

What is the best free study timer app for Android?

If you want something free with no ads, Android's built-in Focus mode in Digital Wellbeing pauses distracting apps, and the Clock app has a timer. Forest, FLIP and YPT are free to download but show ads and sell in-app purchases.

Which study timer apps have no ads?

From their Play listings on 15 November 2026: Flora and Flip to Focus don't show ads, and Android's Digital Wellbeing doesn't either. Forest, FLIP and YPT show ads.

Is there a study timer that works when you flip your phone over?

Yes. FLIP by RinaSoft and Flip to Focus both time study while the phone lies face down. FLIP adds study groups and rankings; Flip to Focus has no ads, no account and collects no data.

Which study app is best for studying with friends?

YPT and FLIP have live study groups and rankings, Forest has Plant Together rooms, and Flora has groups. Flip to Focus has no social features, though you can share a study-time image.

Do study timer apps collect data?

Most do, according to their own Google Play Data safety labels. Check the listing's Data safety section before installing. Flip to Focus's label says no data is collected or shared.
