The best tarot card of the day apps for Android

Updated 15 November 2026 · 8 min read
Short answerFor learning tarot, Labyrinthos Tarot Cards is the most complete free option: ad-free, with lessons, journals and many spreads, paid for by optional purchases. For the original Pamela Colman Smith art and Waite's own guidebook, Tarot! by The Fool's Dog costs $3.99 with no ads. Galaxy Tarot is a long-running free app with ads. Daily Oracle, which we make, is the narrowest: a one-minute daily card from the 1909 deck with a Stoic quote and the real moon, free with ads and no account.

We make Daily Oracle, one of the four apps compared here. We've tried to be fair: where another app is the better choice, we say so. Details about the other apps come from their Google Play listings, checked on 15 November 2026.

Search Google Play for “tarot” and you'll find hundreds of apps, from beautifully made learning tools to thin wrappers around a random number generator and a lot of ads. If what you want is a card of the day (one card each morning to reflect on) the choice gets easier. Here are four Android apps worth considering, what each costs, and who each one suits.

What to look for in a card of the day app

  • The deck. Do you want the classic Rider-Waite-Smith images, or an app's own art? Learning on Pamela Colman Smith's images transfers to most books and decks; learning on a custom deck is lovely if you like the art.
  • How the daily card is chosen. Some apps draw a personal random card; others give everyone the same card on the same day. Random draws repeat more often than people expect: a repeat within a week happens about a quarter of the time.
  • The writing. A short, thoughtful reflection beats a generic paragraph. Look for apps that encourage reflection rather than promising predictions.
  • Journaling. Some apps let you write notes in the app; others give you a question and leave the writing to you.
  • Money and ads. Free with ads, free with purchases, or paid up front. Check whether the features you want are behind a purchase.
  • Account and privacy. Does the app need a sign-up? Where is your history stored?

Labyrinthos Tarot Cards: best for learning tarot

Labyrinthos Academy's app is the most feature-rich of the four and, unusually, has no ads. Its listing describes a daily tarot reading spread among dozens of others (from a single card to the Celtic Cross, plus spreads for each moon phase), an in-app journal where you can add notes to readings, a library of meanings, and lessons and quizzes covering tarot, Lenormand and runes. You can switch reversals on or off, set reminders, and see a “mirror” summary of which cards and suits come up most often.

The app uses Labyrinthos's own deck designs rather than the Rider-Waite-Smith art, and the company sells printed versions of those decks. It's paid for by optional in-app purchases, including AI readings and decks, from $0.99 to $89.99 per item. With over a million downloads and a 4.8 rating, it's the one we'd suggest to someone who wants to study tarot seriously on their phone.

Choose it if you want to learn tarot properly, like journaling inside the app, and don't mind a non-classic deck. Skip it if you want something that takes one minute and nothing more.

Tarot! by The Fool's Dog: best for the original art

The Fool's Dog publishes tarot deck apps. Its “Tarot!” app is a digital restoration of what it calls the Classic 1910 Tarot, Pamela Colman Smith's illustrations in their early colours, with high-resolution images you can zoom into. It includes three complete books: Waite's original guide, The Pictorial Key to the Tarot; S. L. MacGregor Mathers's The Tarot; and a modern guide, The Concise Tarot Reader by Janet Farrar and Gavin Bone.

It has 21 built-in layouts, a free-form layout, a journal, optional reversals and a Major Arcana-only option. Its listing doesn't mention a dedicated card of the day, so for a daily pull you'd draw a single card yourself. It costs $3.99 with no ads and no in-app purchases listed, and has a 4.9 rating from a smaller number of reviews.

Choose it if you love Smith's art, want Waite's own text, and prefer paying once to seeing ads. Skip it if you want a ready-made daily card with a short reflection.

Galaxy Tarot: a long-running free option

Galaxy Tarot by Galaxy Tone Software has been on Google Play since 2010 and has over a million downloads. Its listing highlights a personalised card of the day, interpretations aimed at self-care and personal growth, information on each card's symbolism and a digital deck meant to feel like a physical one. It's free with ads, with in-app purchases from $0.99 to $14.99 per item.

Two things to weigh: its Google Play rating is 3.5 from about 47,000 reviews, noticeably lower than the others, and its last update listed on Google Play was 26 October 2025, over a year before we checked. Read recent reviews before installing.

Choose it if you want a familiar, free, personal daily card and don't mind ads. Skip it if recent reviews raise concerns that matter to you.

Daily Oracle: best for a one-minute ritual

Daily Oracle is our app, and it does less than the others on purpose. It's built around a one-minute morning ritual. Each day it shows one card from scans of the original 1909 Rider-Waite-Smith deck, with a short reflection and a journaling question written for that card; one quote from Marcus Aurelius or Epictetus (365 in all, in George Long's translations); one small action to tick off; and the moon for your date (phase, percent lit, age, moon sign, and the next full and new moon), calculated on your phone with Jean Meeus's astronomical formulas.

Some design choices are different from the other apps here. Everyone who opens it on the same local date gets the same card, and cards follow a fixed shuffle for each year, so a card comes back about every 78 days and never sooner than 40. There's a separate personal 3-card spread (where you are, what to tend, a way forward), a history of every card with a streak, share images for stories and posts, and one optional daily reminder that never gives the card away. Everything works offline, there's no account, and your history stays on your phone.

It's free, with no purchases. It's supported by ads: a small banner on the Today and History screens, a short optional video before the 3-card spread, and at most one full-screen ad a day, only after you share. It's Android only (8.0 and later). It doesn't have lessons, an in-app text journal or a choice of decks; if you want those, Labyrinthos or The Fool's Dog will suit you better.

Choose it if you want a calm daily minute with the classic deck, a Stoic line and accurate moon data, with no sign-up. Skip it if you want to study tarot in depth or never see ads.

Or skip the app: a paper deck and a notebook

None of these apps is necessary. A Rider-Waite-Smith deck, or any deck you like, and a cheap notebook make an excellent daily practice, with no ads, no screens and no battery. Many people use both: a paper deck at home and an app when travelling. Our guide to doing a daily tarot card pull works either way, and the tarot journaling prompts give you something to write.

A word on “psychic” and prediction apps

Some tarot apps promise to reveal your future, tell you what someone is thinking, or connect you with a psychic for a fee per minute. We'd be cautious. Tarot is best used as a prompt for reflection, a way to look at your own situation from a new angle, not as a forecast. Be especially careful with apps that push paid readings when you're upset, or that ask for personal details you wouldn't share with a stranger. For serious decisions about health, money, relationships or the law, talk to a qualified person.

Questions

What is the best free tarot app for Android?

For learning, Labyrinthos Tarot Cards is free and ad-free, paid for by optional purchases. For a quick daily ritual with the classic deck, Daily Oracle is free with ads and no account. Galaxy Tarot is free with ads and purchases.

Which tarot app uses the original Rider-Waite-Smith deck?

Tarot! by The Fool's Dog ($3.99) offers a restored version of the 1910 deck, and Daily Oracle (free with ads) uses scans of the 1909 first printing. Labyrinthos uses its own deck designs.

Do tarot card of the day apps give everyone the same card?

Most draw a personal random card. Daily Oracle gives everyone the same card on the same local date, following a fixed yearly shuffle, so you can compare with a friend; it also has a personal 3-card spread.

Is there a tarot app without ads?

Yes. Labyrinthos Tarot Cards describes itself as 100% ad-free and is funded by optional purchases, and Tarot! by The Fool's Dog is a $3.99 paid app with no ads listed. Daily Oracle and Galaxy Tarot show ads.

Can a tarot app predict the future?

No. We treat tarot as a tool for reflection, not prediction. Be cautious with apps that promise to reveal the future or sell psychic readings.
