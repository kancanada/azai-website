A Plaud alternative without another device

Updated 3 November 2026 · 7 min read
Short answerThe phone you already carry can do much of what a Plaud recorder does. On a Pixel, Google Recorder transcribes on the phone for free. On recent Galaxy phones, Samsung Voice Recorder transcribes and summarizes with Galaxy AI, and Samsung's Phone app can record calls. Otter works on any phone and joins video calls, in its cloud. VoiceVault, which we make, transcribes and summarizes on any Android 13+ phone with no account, for a one-time price. Plaud is still the better choice for recording calls by clipping a device to your phone, or for an all-day wearable.

We make VoiceVault, one of the apps in this guide. We haven't tested Plaud's devices; facts about Plaud and the other apps come from their own published pages, linked below. We've tried to be fair, including where Plaud is the better choice.

Plaud's recorders are well made and popular, but they aren't the only way to get AI meeting notes. If you're looking for a Plaud alternative, it's usually for one of four reasons: you don't want to buy and charge another device, you don't want a subscription once you pass the free minutes, you'd rather your audio didn't go to a cloud, or you're not sure you need it at all. This guide compares the phone apps that do a similar job, and says when Plaud is still worth it.

What you'd be replacing

On 3 November 2026, Plaud's recorders cost $159 (Plaud Note and NotePin), $179 (NotePin S) and $189 (Note Pro). Each includes the free Starter plan with 300 transcription minutes a month. More minutes need Pro ($99.99 a year, 1,200 minutes a month) or Unlimited ($239.99 a year). Plaud's Trust Center says all data is transferred to the cloud for processing; audio and transcripts are only stored there if you turn on Cloud Sync. For a side-by-side with Otter, see Plaud vs Otter.

Google Recorder (Pixel)

If you have a Pixel, try this first. Recorder has transcribed on the phone since it launched, and works in airplane mode. Google's help pages list speaker labels on Pixel 6 and later, and summaries on Pixel 8 and later for recordings of about 5 minutes to an hour in a handful of languages. Google says summaries use a large language model running in a secure, isolated cloud environment, so they aren't fully on the phone. It's free, but only on Pixels.

Samsung Voice Recorder with Galaxy AI

On select Galaxy phones with One UI 6.1 or higher, Voice Recorder's Transcript Assist can transcribe, translate and summarize a recording, and suggest calendar events and reminders. Samsung also says Galaxy phones on Android 15 with One UI 7 or higher can record phone calls in the Samsung Phone app and transcribe and summarize them, with an option to process data only on the device. Samsung notes that call transcription needs a Samsung account and an internet connection, and isn't available on some A-series models. If you have a recent Galaxy, this is the closest free match to the Plaud Note's call recording.

Otter

Otter works on any phone and on the web, and its bot joins Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet. That makes it the better Plaud alternative if most meetings are online. Like Plaud, it transcribes in its cloud, needs an account and an internet connection to transcribe. The free plan gives 300 minutes a month with a 30-minute limit per conversation; Pro is $8.33 a month billed yearly for 1,200 minutes. More in our Otter alternative guide.

VoiceVault (Android)

VoiceVault, which we make, is built for meetings in the room. You put the phone on the table, and it records, transcribes, labels speakers and lists to-dos with owners on the phone. There's no account and no VoiceVault server, and it works in airplane mode. Summaries are made on the phone, on phones that support Gemini Nano or VoiceVault's downloadable on-device model. It keeps recording with the screen off and the phone in your pocket. It's free for 30 days, then a one-time US$49.99, with no minute limits.

What it doesn't do: record phone calls, join video calls, or recognize the same person across recordings. Speaker labels are per recording.

When Plaud is still the better choice

A phone app isn't a straight swap for everyone. Plaud makes more sense if:

  • You record a lot of mobile calls and don't have a recent Galaxy. The Plaud Note records calls through a vibration conduction sensor when it's attached to the back of your phone.
  • You want something you wear all day. The NotePin can be worn as a pin, clip, wristband or necklace. Plaud lists 20 hours of continuous recording and a weight of 0.59 oz. (Plaud says it doesn't record phone calls.)
  • You need a long battery away from a charger. Plaud lists up to 30 hours of continuous recording for the Note, and up to 50 hours in the Note Pro's Endurance mode.
  • You'd rather not use your phone in the meeting. One press on the device starts recording.
  • You use an iPhone and don't want Otter. The Android apps here won't help.
  • You need many languages. Plaud lists 112 transcription languages.

How to choose

  1. Mostly online meetings? Otter.
  2. Mostly mobile phone calls? Samsung's Phone app on a recent Galaxy, or the Plaud Note.
  3. Mostly in-person meetings, on a Pixel? Start with Google Recorder.
  4. Mostly in-person, confidential, any Android 13+ phone, and no subscription? VoiceVault. See AI notes for confidential meetings.

Whatever you pick, the law on recording doesn't change: in some US states everyone has to agree. See one-party and all-party consent states. And if you're still weighing the device itself, read what the Plaud Note does and costs.

Questions

What is the best free Plaud alternative?

If you have a Pixel, Google Recorder: it's built in and transcribes on the phone. On a recent Samsung Galaxy, Voice Recorder with Galaxy AI's Transcript Assist, whose basic features Samsung says are free. Otter's free plan works on any phone but gives 300 minutes a month and stops each conversation at 30 minutes.

Can my phone record calls like the Plaud Note?

Some can. Samsung says Galaxy phones on Android 15 with One UI 7 or higher can record calls in the Samsung Phone app, and transcribe and summarize them with Transcript Assist. The Plaud Note records calls through a sensor when attached to the back of your phone. Otter and VoiceVault don't record phone calls.

Is there a Plaud alternative without a subscription?

Yes. Google Recorder and Samsung's Voice Recorder are built into their phones. VoiceVault is free for 30 days, then a one-time US$49.99, with no minute limits. Plaud's own free Starter plan has no subscription either, but it's limited to 300 transcription minutes a month.

Which Plaud alternative keeps audio on the phone?

Google Recorder transcribes on the phone, though Google says its summaries run in a secure cloud environment. Samsung says its Transcript Assist needs a network connection and a Samsung account. VoiceVault makes transcripts and summaries on the phone with no account. Plaud's Trust Center says all Plaud data goes to the cloud for processing.

Can I use these apps on an iPhone?

Google Recorder, Samsung Voice Recorder and VoiceVault are Android only. Otter has iPhone and Android apps.
