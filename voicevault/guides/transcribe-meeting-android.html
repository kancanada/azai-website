How to transcribe an in-person meeting on Android

Updated 31 October 2026 · 8 min read
Short answerPut your phone flat in the middle of the table, microphone end towards the people talking, and tell everyone you're recording. Use a recorder that transcribes: Google Recorder on a Pixel, Samsung Voice Recorder with Galaxy AI on a recent Galaxy, a cloud app such as Otter, or an on-device app such as VoiceVault. Afterwards, check the speaker names and the to-dos before you share anything.

We make VoiceVault, which we use for the step-by-step part of this guide. The recording tips work with any app.

Most bad transcripts are bad recordings. A phone in the right spot, in a quiet-enough room, gets you most of the way. The rest is picking an app that fits your meetings and spending two minutes checking the result before you send it to anyone.

Before the meeting

1. Tell people you're recording

It's courteous, it's often required, and people speak more clearly when they know. Follow the consent rules where you are and your organization's policy: see recording a meeting in Canada and one-party and all-party consent states in the US.

2. Put the phone in the right place

  • Flat, in the middle of the table. Every speaker should be within about two metres.
  • Microphone uncovered. On most phones the main microphone is on the bottom edge. Don't put it under papers or a notebook.
  • Away from noise. Laptop fans, projectors, air vents and clinking cups all end up in the transcript as gaps or nonsense.
  • Big room? Seat yourself in the middle, or ask people at the far end to speak up. A second phone at the other end beats one phone straining.

3. Check battery and notifications

An hour of recording and transcription uses a noticeable amount of battery. Start above 30% or plug in. Turn on Do Not Disturb so notification sounds don't land in the recording.

After the meeting

  • Read the summary before you share it. AI summaries are good at the gist and occasionally wrong on names and numbers. Fix what matters.
  • Confirm owners on the to-dos. "We should..." often means nobody. Assign it.
  • Pick the right summary style. VoiceVault has styles for meetings, 1:1s, client calls, interviews, lectures, doctor visits and brainstorms, or your own prompt.
  • Keep a backup if you need one. VoiceVault can copy recordings to your own Google Drive, and exports to PDF, Word and Markdown.

Troubleshooting

Words are missing or wrong

Usually distance or noise. Move the phone closer to the quietest speaker, and away from fans. Accents and jargon improve when people speak a little slower.

Two people get the same speaker label

Similar voices, or people talking over each other, can confuse speaker detection. In VoiceVault, press and hold a line to fix the name on it.

The meeting switched language

VoiceVault transcribes 25 European languages, including English, French, German and Spanish. For other languages, check what your app supports before the meeting.

Questions

Where should I put my phone to record a meeting?

Flat on the table, in the middle of the group, within about two metres of the furthest speaker, with nothing covering the bottom edge where the main microphone usually is. Keep it away from laptop fans and coffee cups.

Can Android transcribe a recording without internet?

Yes, with an app that runs speech recognition on the phone: Google Recorder on Pixels, Samsung Voice Recorder with Galaxy AI on recent Galaxy phones, or VoiceVault on Android 13 and newer. Cloud apps such as Otter need a connection to transcribe.

How much storage does an hour of meeting audio use?

It depends on the app's audio format. VoiceVault saves compressed AAC audio at about 22 MB per hour, so even a busy month of meetings takes little space.

Can I record with the screen off?

In most recorder apps, yes. VoiceVault keeps recording and transcribing with the screen off and the phone in your pocket; a notification shows it's recording.

Do I need permission to record a meeting?

Tell people you're recording, and follow the consent rules where you are and your organization's policy. Rules differ by country and province or state.
