AI meeting notes have become a monthly bill. Most people don't notice it until they add up the year, or until a busy month runs into a minute limit. This guide explains why the subscriptions exist, what they cost over time, and when you don't need one.

Why AI note takers charge every month

Cloud note takers send your recording to their servers, where large AI models transcribe it, work out who spoke, and write a summary. That computing costs real money for every minute of audio, so the price follows usage: monthly plans with a set number of minutes, or higher tiers that remove the cap.

An app that runs the AI on your phone has no server bill per minute. Your phone does the work, using power you've already paid for. That's why on-device apps can be sold once.

When a subscription is worth it

Paying monthly makes sense if you use what the servers make possible:

  • Bots in online meetings. Otter and Fireflies can join Zoom, Meet and Teams calls without you being there.
  • Team workspaces. Shared notes, comments, integrations with Slack, CRMs and project tools.
  • Every device. Web, iPhone and Android with the same account.
  • Sales and coaching analytics. Talk time, sentiment and topic tracking across a team.

When paying once makes more sense

  • Most of your meetings happen in a room, not on a video call.
  • You record a lot, and minute limits or per-meeting caps get in the way.
  • Some meetings are confidential, and you'd rather they never leave your phone.
  • You want notes on a plane, in a basement, or anywhere the signal drops.

What to check before buying a one-time app

  1. Does it really work offline? Turn on airplane mode and record a short test. If the transcript only appears later, it's using a server.
  2. Does it need an account? An app with no account has nowhere to keep your recordings except your phone.
  3. What's the one-time download? On-device AI models are large. Set the app up on Wi-Fi.
  4. Is there a trial, and what happens after it? You should keep what you've already recorded.
  5. Does your phone meet the requirements? VoiceVault needs Android 13 or newer; summaries are fastest on recent phones.

Questions

Why do AI note takers need a subscription?

Because they transcribe and summarize your audio on their own servers, which costs them money for every minute you record. Apps that run the AI on your phone don't have that cost.

Is there a free AI note taker with no limits?

Built-in apps come close if you have the right phone: Google Recorder on Pixel phones and Samsung Voice Recorder with Galaxy AI on recent Galaxy phones. They're free but tied to those brands, and their features vary by model.

What do I lose with a one-time-purchase app?

Usually online-meeting bots, team sharing and web access. On-device apps also need a capable phone and a one-time AI download, and long summaries take longer on older phones.
